= A cancellation racing a dispatch
:description: The customer cancels a confirmed order at the moment the warehouse hands the parcel to the carrier.

== The scenario

A customer cancels an order that is already confirmed, at the same moment the warehouse hands that parcel to the carrier. Both acts are legitimate, and neither side can see the other. The order is past placement, so a shipment exists, and dispatch is about to become irreversible.

== What would go wrong

The old design treated cancellation as a command. Order-service marked the order cancelled and told catalog and shipping afterwards. If the parcel had already left in that same moment, three records disagreed: the order said cancelled, the stock had been released, and the parcel was on its way. The late dispatch event then landed in a dead-letter topic, because both order-service and catalog refused it.

== The fix

Dispatch is the point of no return, and shipping is the owner of that decision, because only shipping knows whether the parcel has left. A confirmed cancellation is a request, not a command: the order moves to `CANCELLATION_REQUESTED` and the customer receives `202`, which means the request was taken and the outcome is not known yet. Shipping then cancels the shipment, or refuses because it has already been dispatched. The two attempts on one shipment are serialised by optimistic locking; the loser is retried and sees the winner, so the system ends either cancelled with the stock released, or shipped with the stock committed.

== How it is proven

* `CancellationRaceIT` (shipping, 20 rounds)
* `CancellationRaceE2EIT` (whole system, 8 rounds, dead-letter topics stay empty)

Firing both at once (`race`) let the dispatch win three times: cancel returned `202`, dispatch `200`, the order was `SHIPPED`, the shipment `DISPATCHED`, and the stock was committed. Waiting two seconds before the dispatch (`race 2`) let the request win: dispatch returned `409`, the order and the shipment were `CANCELLED`, and the reserved unit was available again.
