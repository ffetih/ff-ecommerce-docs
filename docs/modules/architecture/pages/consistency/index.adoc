= Consistency: seven failure stories
:description: Each rule in the saga exists because of one concrete way the system could end up wrong.

Every service is consistent on its own. The hard part is between them: a message can arrive late,
twice, or not at all, and two things can happen at the same moment in two services. Each story below
is one such situation, what would have gone wrong, what the system does instead, and the test that
proves it.

[cols="1,2"]
|===
|Story |Situation

|xref:consistency/cancel-dispatch-race.adoc[A cancellation racing a dispatch]
|The customer cancels a confirmed order at the moment the warehouse hands the parcel to the carrier.

|xref:consistency/delivery-without-dispatch.adoc[A delivery without a dispatch]
|`shipment-delivered` reaches order-service for an order that never became SHIPPED.

|xref:consistency/stuck-orders.adoc[Orders that stop moving]
|An event the next step needs ends in a dead-letter topic, and the order waits forever.

|xref:consistency/silent-failures.adoc[Failures nobody sees]
|Events stop reaching Kafka, or a consumer gives up on one, and nothing tells anyone.

|xref:consistency/replayed-order-placed.adoc[A replayed order-placed]
|`order-placed` arrives again after the order was cancelled and its reservation released, and the inbox no longer recognises it.

|xref:consistency/replayed-order-confirmed.adoc[A replayed order-confirmed]
|An order's confirmation was dead-lettered, shipping accepted its cancellation without a shipment, and later someone replays the dead letter.

|xref:consistency/inbox-forgets.adoc[An inbox that forgets too early]
|A consumer group's offsets are lost (they expire while the service is down, or someone resets them), and Kafka delivers everything still in the topic again.
|===

Each story is told three times, from three angles: *why* it is designed this way (here), *break it
and watch it* (xref:developer:try-it/failure-drills.adoc[failure drills]), and *what to do when it
happens* (xref:operations:dead-letters.adoc[runbook]).
