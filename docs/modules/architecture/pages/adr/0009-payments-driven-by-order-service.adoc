= ADR-0009: Payments, driven by order-service
:description: Where payment enters the order flow, who drives it, and how money stays correct when the provider is slow or silent.

* *Status:* Accepted (implemented in s08)
* *Date:* 2026-11-26

== Context

Orders now need payment. A card payment has three moves: *authorize* (the bank holds the amount;
the hold expires after some days, typically about a week), *capture* (the money moves) and *void*
(the hold is released before capture); after capture only a *refund* gives money back.

Payment adds a fourth participant to the saga (ADR-0007), and the first one where a mistake costs
money directly. It also brings a failure the other participants do not have: the provider may not
answer, and then the outcome is unknown rather than failed. Charging twice, or capturing for an
order that was cancelled, are the errors to rule out.

ADR-0007 chose choreography and named payments as the moment to revisit it.

== Decision

*Order of steps.* Stock is reserved first, then the payment is authorized; the order becomes
`CONFIRMED` only when both have happened. No hold is placed for goods we do not have, and a
reservation waits at most a short time for its payment.

----
place ─► reserve stock ─► authorize payment ─► CONFIRMED ─► dispatch ─► capture
              │ no stock        │ declined                       │
              ▼                 ▼                                 └─► capture fails: payment overdue,
          CANCELLED     release stock, CANCELLED                       the order stays SHIPPED
cancelled after authorization, before capture: void
----

*Capture at dispatch.* Money moves when the goods leave, the usual card-scheme rule. Dispatch is the
point of no return (ADR-0007), so a capture that fails afterwards does not cancel anything: the order
stays `SHIPPED`, the payment becomes overdue and visible to operations.

*order-service drives the flow.* order-service already decides every step of the order's state.
From now on it also *commands* the new participant: after `stock-reserved` it sends
`authorize-payment`, after `shipment-dispatched` it sends `capture-payment`, and after a
cancellation of an authorized order it sends `void-payment`; payment-service answers with events.
The steps, the waiting points and the compensations of the payment part are written in one place.
Catalog and shipping keep their event contracts from s06; moving them to commands as well would
change working, proven code without adding a guarantee, and can follow later if the flow grows.

*One payment per order, idempotent everywhere.* The payment's identity is the order id. Every
command is processed once (inbox); every call to the provider carries an idempotency key derived
from the payment and the operation, so a retry can never charge twice.

*Unknown is not declined.* A provider call that times out leaves the payment *pending*: the money may
or may not be held. payment-service asks again with the same idempotency key until the provider
gives an answer; only an explicit decline cancels the order. Order expiry (ADR-0007) bounds the wait:
a confirmed order is asked to cancel after 3 days, before a typical hold expires, and a cancellation
voids the hold.

*A double-entry ledger.* Every money movement (capture, refund) is booked twice, as a debit and a
credit of the same amount, in the same transaction as the payment's change. The sum of all entries is
always zero; a report that does not balance is a bug, found at once. An authorization moves no money
and books nothing.

*A simulated provider.* The payment provider is a port with a simulated adapter in payment-service.
The card token on the order chooses its behaviour (approve, decline, time out), so tests and drills
can produce every outcome on purpose.

== Consequences

* The payment part of the flow is readable in order-service as a sequence, including its
  compensations; the rest stays as documented in ADR-0007.
* order-service knows payment's commands; payment-service knows nothing about catalog or shipping.
* Orders carry a payment method (a card token); the web console needs a field for it.
* New failure stories: a provider that does not answer, a cancellation racing an authorization, and a
  capture repeated by a retry. Each gets a test and a drill, like the seven of s06.
* A refund needs a return first; it follows with returns (s09).

== Implementation notes (s08)

Decisions taken while building it, within the decision above:

* *The order records the payment's state* (`paymentStatus`: `NOT_REQUESTED`, `AUTHORIZING`,
  `AUTHORIZED`, `DECLINED`, `VOIDING`, `VOIDED`, `CAPTURING`, `CAPTURED`, `OVERDUE`) next to its own
  status, rather than a new order status. The order lifecycle customers see did not change; it stays
  `PLACED` until the authorization arrives.
* *Commands follow from state changes.* The order's state machine moves the payment into an -ING
  state; one rule turns that change into the command and writes it to the outbox in the same
  transaction. Customer cancellation, a declined card, shipping's answers and order expiry all reach
  payment through this one rule, so none of them can forget a void.
* *Expiry bounds an unanswered authorization at 30 minutes*, not 3 days: the order is still `PLACED`,
  and expiring it voids the pending authorization.
* *payment-service remembers a void* that arrives while the authorization is unanswered, and asks
  the provider again at once (xref:consistency/cancel-during-authorization.adoc[story]).
* *A void the provider refuses* is logged and treated as done: no money was taken, and the hold lapses
  at the provider on its own.
* *The ledger enforces its rules in the database*: a statement-level trigger rejects a transaction
  whose entries do not sum to zero in one currency, row triggers reject updates and deletes, and a
  unique reference makes booking idempotent.
* *payment-service has no public API.* Its state is visible on the order (`paymentStatus`) and, for
  drills, in its database.

== Alternatives considered

* *Choreography, as today.* payment-service would react to `stock-reserved` and order-service to
  `payment-authorized`. It works, but payment then depends on catalog's events, and the compensation
  order (declined: release stock, then cancel) exists only as reactions spread over services.
* *Full orchestration now.* Catalog and shipping would also receive commands. Cleaner on paper, but it
  rewrites the participants whose behaviour s06 just proved, for no new guarantee.
* *Capture at confirmation.* Simpler, but takes the customer's money before the goods exist on a
  truck, and turns every later cancellation into a refund.
* *Authorize before reserving.* Avoids reserving stock for customers who cannot pay, but places holds
  for goods we may not have; declines are rarer than they are expensive to explain.
