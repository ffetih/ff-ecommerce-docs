= Domain model: contexts and language
:description: The bounded contexts of the shop, the words each one uses, and how they relate.

The shop is not one model but six, each with its own words and rules. Domain-driven design calls
them *bounded contexts*: inside one, every word has exactly one meaning; between them, the same word
may mean different things, and the boundary is where one meaning is translated into the other.

== Bounded contexts

[cols="1,1,1,3"]
|===
|Context |Service |Kind |Responsible for

|Ordering |order-service |core
|What the customer bought, at which prices, and where the order stands. The heart of the business.

|Catalog & inventory |catalog-service |core
|What can be sold, at which price, and how many units are free, promised or gone. Kept together on
purpose: a reservation must check price, availability and status in one place
(xref:architecture:adr/0003-catalog-and-inventory-in-one-service.adoc[ADR-0003]).

|Shipping |shipping-service |supporting
|The parcel: preparing, handing over to the carrier, delivery. Owns the point of no return.

|Payment |payment-service |supporting
|The customer's money: holding it on the card, taking it when the parcel leaves, releasing it after
a cancellation, and booking every movement in a double-entry ledger. Ordering decides *when*;
Payment knows *how*, and what the provider answered.

|Customer |customer-service |supporting
|Who the shop delivers to: name, shipping address, whether the customer may still order.

|Identity |Keycloak |generic
|Who someone is and what they may do. Bought, not built: login is not where this shop differs.
|===

*Core* contexts are where the business competes and where the effort goes; *supporting* ones are
needed but ordinary; *generic* ones are taken off the shelf.

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Relationship |Meaning here

|Conformist
|Ordering and Customer accept Identity's model as it is: the Keycloak user id *is* the customer id.
No mapping table.

|Customer/supplier, synchronous, with an anticorruption layer
|Ordering asks Customer and Catalog while placing an order, and translates their answers into its
own small types (`CustomerSnapshot`, `ProductPrice`). Their full models never leak into Ordering.

|Published language (events)
|Everything after placing travels as events defined in one shared module (`events`), the only
shared code between contexts. Each consumer reads only what it needs.

|Customer/supplier, asynchronous commands
|Ordering tells Payment what to do (`authorize-payment`, `capture-payment`, `void-payment`), and Payment
answers with events (xref:architecture:adr/0009-payments-driven-by-order-service.adoc[ADR-0009]).
Payment knows nothing about stock or parcels: only an order id, an amount and a card token.
|===

== Ubiquitous language: one word, several meanings

The same word means something different in each context. Inside a context, use its meaning
exactly; across contexts, say which one you mean.

[cols="1,2,2,2,2"]
|===
|Word |Ordering |Catalog & inventory |Shipping |Payment

|*order*
|the customer's purchase: lines, frozen prices, total, status
|only an id: the key of a reservation
|only an id: the key of a shipment
|only an id and an amount: one payment per order

|*price*
|the unit price *at the moment of placing*, frozen in the order line
|the *current* unit price of a product; may change any time
|not known
|the *amount* to hold and take, exactly the order's total

|*cancel*
|PLACED: cancel at once. CONFIRMED: *request* cancellation
|*release* the reservation: units become available again
|*cancel the shipment*, only before dispatch
|*void*: release the hold; after capture only a refund gives money back

|*return*
|goods of a delivered order sent back: lines, frozen prices, a reason; counted per line
|units *received* back: stock on hand again, booked as a goods receipt
|not involved: the customer sends the parcel
|a *refund*: money back for one return, never more than was captured

|*customer*
|a snapshot taken when placing: id, active, address
|not known
|an id and a delivery address on the parcel
|an id and a card token

|*product / line*
|SKU, quantity, frozen unit price, line total
|SKU, name, current price, active; stock per SKU
|SKU and quantity to pack
|not known
|===

Two more distinctions the language keeps sharp:

* *Reserved* vs *committed* stock: reserved units are promised but still in the warehouse; committed
  units have left with the parcel.
* *Authorized* vs *captured* money: an authorization only holds the amount on the card, and nothing
  is booked; a capture takes it and is booked in the ledger. *Declined* (the provider said no) is not
  *unknown* (the provider did not answer).
* *Deactivated customer* (Customer: may not order) vs *disabled user* (Identity: may not log in):
  two decisions in two contexts.

The definitions of every term are in the xref:glossary.adoc[glossary]. How the code protects each
context's rules: xref:architecture:aggregates.adoc[aggregates and invariants].
